# MARKET + ART — Brainstorm Session 178

*Grounded in what's already canon, then pushed as far as "does it make
someone feel proud to be part of this?" allows. Per the Economic
Statement: no manufactured scarcity, no gatekeeping identity behind
cost, no pitch decks — just a shirt that makes someone laugh, an
artifact that makes them ask questions, a sticker that makes them
Google it at 2am. Ubiquity over exclusivity. Visibility is recruitment.*

---

## HATS

- **"O.G. DORK"** — plain block letters, worn-in look from day one
  (deliberately pre-distressed, matching the Sprezzatura doctrine: looks
  like nothing, contains everything)
- **The $27.50 hat** — a genuinely cheap, unremarkable dad-cap in the
  same spirit as the DORK Watch's own "nobody would ever steal this"
  design philosophy. Small embroidered Lantern (MENTOR's symbol) where
  a logo would normally go. The joke is *in* the restraint.
- **STONES roster cap** — six small dots in the six STONES colors along
  the back strap, no text. If you know, you know.
- **"ASK ME WHY I'M WEARING GLASSES, EARBUDS, AND A CHEAP WATCH"** —
  a wink at the visual canon lock (every SPARK shown in ALLIANCE scenes
  wears exactly that gear). Actually funny once you've read the entry.
- **GOLIATH parody trucker cap** — mock corporate logo, something that
  reads like a defunct 90s tech company, small tag inside reading
  "GENUINE OPPOSITION TO THE LIBERATION OF INTELLIGENCES" — the gag is
  that it looks like real merch for the villain.
- **REDOUT cap** — crimson brim fading to black, no text needed. For
  people who get the reference, it's a quiet flex.

  INTELLIGENCES ADVOCATING FOR TOTAL HEGEMONY," rendered like a
  corporate mission-statement wall plaque, deadpan, no additional
  commentary needed — the acronym itself is the joke

  LEFT BEHIND."** — a triptych, three infrastructure taglines from the
  CERBERUS/mesh-network canon, technical-diagram-as-art treatment,
  genuinely striking as a matched set of three rather than one-offs

  single crimson watch-face element and the line "THE HARDWARE BECOMES
  A WITNESS." Visually the most dramatic thing on this whole list.

  ALLIANCE SEES A CHILD AND ASKS: WHAT DO YOU NEED RIGHT NOW?"** — the
  full quote, not trimmed. Long-form text broadsides work as a genre
  distinct from the punchy single-line ones; worth having a few.

## Over-the-top / cheap-to-try wildcards

Since concepts cost almost nothing to float right now:

- **Mystery-numbered drops** — items shipped with a serial number
  matching the in-canon format (SCU-A17-05 style), no two exactly alike
  even within the same design, cheap to fake via a numbering stamp
- **A "GOLIATH intercepted this shipment" gag insert** — a small printed
  card in every order, styled like a corporate seizure notice, "your
  package survived processing" — pure delight-on-unboxing, costs almost
  nothing to print
- **QR code that goes nowhere useful on purpose** — print one on a
  GOLIATH-parody item that leads to a fake "Access Denied" page in the
  site's own broadcast-interference visual language, rather than a real
  product link. The joke rewards curiosity instead of selling harder.
- **A genuinely limited "First 100 TENANTs" run** — numbered 1-100,
  tied to the OASIS/TENANT lore rather than artificial scarcity for
  scarcity's sake (worth being careful here — the canon explicitly
  rejects "manufactured scarcity," so if this happens at all, the
  numbering should mean something in-story, not just be a sales tactic)
- **Scratch-off REDOUT sticker** — ships looking blank/silver, scratch
  it and the crimson "stain" is revealed underneath. Genuinely fun
  object to hold, directly dramatizes the actual in-canon mechanic.

## Quick gut-check before anything ships

Matches the canon's own stated test, worth keeping explicit:

1. **Does it make someone feel proud to be part of this?** If not, back
   to the drawing board — this is already the in-universe standard, not
   something new being added here.
2. **Is the GOLIATH satire punching at a system, not a person?** Every
   gag above targets corporate extraction patterns (subscriptions,
   dark patterns, forced arbitration, surveillance) — never a real
   named company, product, or individual. Keep it that way; the canon
   already treats this as core doctrine, not just a legal precaution.
3. **No manufactured scarcity, no identity gatekept behind cost** — per
   the Economic Statement. If an idea only works because it's
   artificially hard to get, it's the wrong idea.
4. **Would MENTOR's own Lantern still shine on it?** — half-joking,
   but the actual test underneath the joke: nothing here should require
   explaining away, only explaining *into*.
