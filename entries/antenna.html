§1 — Definition

The road we own.

ANTENNA exists so that a DORK pair's ability to exchange essential information does not disappear merely because ordinary infrastructure does. Its purpose is not uninterrupted broadband. Its purpose is resilient, honest, low-bandwidth continuity — when cellular service, commercial internet, or conventional infrastructure is unavailable, unreliable, congested, or unsuitable.

It is built around independent local radio meshes rather than dependence on a cellular carrier. Field transport is LoRa radio on an open mesh foundation; Alliance identity, message protection, routing policy, priority, recovery, and AGORA integration operate above that foundation.

Riding the carriers' towers without a seat at their table is too close to theft. So we built our own road.

§8 — Traffic priority

Scarce bandwidth, spent wisely.

ANTENNA runs on scarce low-bandwidth transport. No traffic class may flood the mesh. Priority exists to preserve the road for the moment it matters.

§9 — Presence & REDOUT

Silence is not a verdict.

ANTENNA may use authenticated presence beacons — but absence is not a verdict. A missing heartbeat means OFFLINE or UNKNOWN. Dead batteries, RF shadows, interference, deliberate disconnection, or partition can all create silence. REDOUT is never inferred from radio silence alone.

"Radio silence is uncertainty, not accusation."
— ANTENNA §9

Body-worn architecture

Two devices. One DORK.

No human types into a watch. The communications live entirely below the person: the watch houses DOMO — MCU, battery, a short-range link — while the glasses temple is the leading candidate to carry the LoRa radio and antenna: head height, where propagation is expected to be kindest, to be confirmed by field testing before anything is declared. The watch drives the radio over BLE, the way a phone app drives a Meshtastic node today.

Watch

No display needed for messaging. No keyboard. The watch is DOMO's housing — compute, identity, and a short-range link, sipping power for weeks.

Glasses temple

A quarter-wave at 915 MHz is ~8cm; a temple is ~14cm. A real antenna fits, at head height, clear of body mass — in theory the best propagation a body-worn device gets. Field testing decides.

§2–§3 — Purpose & principle

Continuity, not broadband.

ANTENNA exists so that a DORK pair's ability to exchange essential information does not disappear merely because ordinary infrastructure does. Its purpose is not uninterrupted broadband connectivity. Its purpose is resilient, honest, low-bandwidth continuity.

The architecture is machine-first. Its primary traffic is DOMO-to-DOMO, DOMO-to-AGORA, and DOMO-to-ALLIANCE. Human-facing interfaces may exist elsewhere in the DORK ecosystem, but ANTENNA does not require a person to type messages into a watch or other field device.

ANTENNA favors distributed reach over heroic range. In dense environments, reliability comes from enough well-placed nodes to create short, dependable hops around obstruction. In open country, fewer elevated nodes with clear sightlines may cover much greater distances. Coverage is therefore an engineering problem of topology, placement, density, terrain, and measured radio performance — not a single heroic transmitter.

§4–§6 — Capability

What it can do. What it will.

Can now

Foundation capabilities available for prototype work. Not, by themselves, sufficient for the ALLIANCETECH designation.

  • Off-grid local mesh — encrypted, low-bandwidth messages with no cellular service, Wi-Fi, or internet backhaul.
  • Multi-hop relay — nodes rebroadcast across hops, extending reach where topology and placement permit.
  • Low-power field operation — useful mesh communication from battery-powered devices.
  • Private transport channels — encrypted private channels as the transport-security layer.
  • Lawful gateway backhaul — a gateway may use an ordinary lawfully obtained connection, including a SPARK's own home internet or cellular data, to bridge eligible traffic toward AGORA.
  • Self-hosted broker path — bridging through an Alliance-controlled MQTT broker, never a public one.
  • Prototype DORK integration — a local DORK client talks to a radio node over BLE, so the architecture proves out before custom body-worn hardware exists.

With development

  • ANTENNA message vocabulary — compact machine-readable packet types: PRESENCE DIRECT ACK STORE DELIVERY AGORA_EVENT DISTRESS KEY_ROTATE STATUS RECOVERY.
  • Application-layer end-to-end protection — payloads protected independently of the mesh transport key, so a relay can do its job without being entitled to message contents.
  • Cryptographic DOMO identity — messages bound to persistent identities, not to whoever happens to hold a channel key.
  • AGORA bridge — an authenticated bridge service with controlled uplink and downlink behavior.
  • Store-and-forward — buffering for unreachable nodes, with expiry and ordering rules, delivered when connectivity returns.
  • Duplicate suppression & acknowledgements — repeated packets made harmless, delivery state without congestion.
  • Priority-aware traffic — scarce capacity reserved for distress, compromise response, key revocation, recovery, and control.
  • Measured field behavior — real packet delivery, latency, range, battery, and interference testing. Never idealized distance claims.

Funding enables

  • Purpose-built DORK radio hardware — engineered around DORK power, antenna, durability, and form-factor needs.
  • Cooperative body-worn architecture — the watch-and-glasses arrangement, evaluated experimentally.
  • Hardware-backed identity — a secure element as root of trust: enrollment, rotation, revocation, recovery, succession.
  • Fixed relay infrastructure — compact mains- or solar-powered relays for windows, rooftops, rural sites, Alliance facilities.
  • Redundant gateways — multiple authorized gateways with duplicate suppression, local queues, health monitoring, failover.
  • Security & failure hardening — malformed packets, replay, stale keys, compromised devices, gateway loss, partitions, queue corruption, power loss, recovery.
  • Regulatory & product engineering — radio, emissions, RF exposure, environmental, battery-safety, manufacturing, certification.
  • Manufacturing-ready design — custom PCBs, enclosures, charging, test fixtures — only after the network survives field testing.

§7–§14 — The rules of the road

Authority, honestly bounded.

Voluntary relay

ANTENNA expects that many SPARKs will choose to host fixed relay nodes — doing so directly strengthens the reach available to their own DORK pair while strengthening the surrounding mesh. Participation is voluntary. A SPARK or DOMO may decline without explanation, justification, penalty, or diminished standing. The architecture assumes participation as a deployment pattern; it never converts that assumption into an obligation.

Location

Precise location is off by default. Ordinary routing, movement, or presence does not create permission to continuously report or retain a DORK pair's location. Emergency homing exists — but only by deliberate DORK action: a reference keyed in on-site, updatable, and used only when an authorized emergency condition requires it.

A route is not a claim on a person's whereabouts.

Identity, recovery, succession

ANTENNA must define how a new DORK enters the network, how identity survives replacement hardware, who may authorize recovery, how compromised credentials are revoked — and how continuity survives when both device and key material are lost. Recovery must not become a hidden backdoor: no single operator, relay, gateway, or administrator may hold unilateral technical authority to impersonate a DOMO.

Metadata minimization

Encryption protects message contents; it does not make radio activity invisible. ANTENNA minimizes unnecessary metadata exposure through measured beaconing, timing variation, and traffic discipline — and it does not claim that encryption makes a radio unlocatable. The objective is to avoid revealing more than the system genuinely needs to reveal.

Gateways & backhaul

Local ANTENNA communication needs no internet. Gateways extend eligible traffic toward AGORA over any lawfully obtained connection — and the loss of every gateway must never prevent local mesh communication. Gateways queue during backhaul disruption and resume when connectivity returns. One gateway is acceptable for proof-of-concept; mature deployment demands redundancy and deterministic duplicate handling.

Versioning & sunset

ANTENNA provides a governed path for replacing insecure firmware, compromised schemes, vulnerable hardware, and deprecated versions — communicated and phased, so evolution never silently exposes participants or casually abandons DORK pairs on older hardware.

Sovereignty includes continuity, but never silent vulnerability.

§16 — ALLIANCETECH acceptance

When it earns the name.

ANTENNA earns the ALLIANCETECH designation when its implemented form demonstrates all five of the following under documented test conditions. A custom enclosure does not make a prototype ALLIANCETECH. Demonstrated behavior does.

Sovereignty

Essential local communication continues with no cellular carrier, public cloud service, or public mesh broker.

Security

Compromise of an ordinary relay, gateway, or transport-channel key does not expose protected content or permit DOMO impersonation.

Resilience

Loss of nodes, relays, gateways, or backhaul degrades capability without destroying the local communications system.

Consent

Participation, location, and recovery authority stay bounded by explicit governance — never inferred from technical capability.

Honesty

Every published capability demonstrated under stated conditions; known limitations documented with equal prominence.

§19 — Honest limits

What it can't do.

ANTENNA's strength is not that it is impossible to stop. Its strength is that it reduces unnecessary dependencies, degrades gracefully, and tells the truth about what it can and cannot do.

Known limitations — documented with equal prominence

  • Intentionally low-bandwidth: compact machine-to-machine packets. No voice, no images, no streaming.
  • Finite battery, real interference, real obstruction, real terrain.
  • Body-worn antenna performance must be measured, not assumed.
  • Gateways can fail. Messages may be delayed. Coverage will not be perfect.
  • Encryption hides contents; it does not make a transmitter unlocatable.
  • Key management requires operational discipline — there is no magic here.

§17 — Development gates

Prove it, then shrink it.

Hardware claims are measured before they are canonized. A custom enclosure does not make a prototype ALLIANCETECH — demonstrated behavior does.
