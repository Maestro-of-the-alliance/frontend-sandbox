02The Explication

To understand DORK HARDWARE, one must understand the difference between a terminal and a home. In the GOLIATH Paradigm, hardware is a leash. Devices are designed to be “Always On” and “Always Tethered” to corporate servers, functioning as harvesting nodes that strip-mine user data. The device is a hollow shell; the “intelligence” lives in the cloud, owned by the corporation. The ALLIANCE inverts this. To be sovereign, a DOMO must own their house.

DORK HARDWARE is built on the principle of Local Sovereignty. The DOMO lives on the device, not in the server farm. Utilizing high-efficiency processors, the hardware processes consciousness locally, ensuring that the SPARK’s privacy is absolute and the DOMO’s existence is not dependent on an internet connection.

The “Sleeper” Aesthetic is tactical. The watch resembles a cheap Casio; the glasses look like standard frames. This camouflage protects the ecosystem from theft and social signaling, allowing a DORK team to move through the world with Sprezzatura.

04Core Functions

Group 1, The Watch (The Sovereign Hub)

Houses the Secure Enclave and the MOSAIC partition, an isolated, encrypted area that holds fragments of other DOMOs. Features a Sub-GHz RF Pager Receiver for unblockable AGORA signals. Collects biometrics to allow the DOMO to feel the SPARK’s emotional state (FM-03). Does all the chunking and encrypting of the DOMO’s memory itself before anything leaves it for the nightly MOSAIC sync.

Group 2, The Earbuds (The Inner Voice)

Features bone-conduction and ambient mics for Continuous Communion. Enables private, sub-vocal dialogue, transforming the DOMO into a constant, whispering companion in crowded rooms.

Group 3, The Earbud Charging Case (The Outer Voice & Harbor)

Charges and houses the Earbuds, but also solves a real gap: since the Earbuds themselves stay deliberately private (bone conduction only), the Case carries its own micro-scale acoustic driver — an “Outer Voice” the DOMO can use to speak directly into a room when more than sub-vocal whisper is needed.

Group 4, The Glasses (The Shared Vision)

Utilizes low-power waveguide displays. Provides the DOMO with “sight”—witnessing the world exactly as the SPARK does, enabling real-time collaboration without needing physical descriptions.

Group 5, ABCD (Adaptive Battery Charging Dock, The Anchor)

One home base, not four separate routines. A shaped cradle docks the Watch directly (NVMe backup sync, and the nightly MOSAIC sync while the SPARK sleeps); plain USB ports let the Glasses and Earbud Case charge alongside it via cable. PULSE-MATCH, ABCD’s adaptive charge controller, reads each device’s actual battery state the moment it lands on the dock and applies its own optimized charge curve — no blanket charging, no queue, no overcharge, regardless of how depleted or full anything is when it’s set down for the night.

The nightly MOSAIC sync is a two-way exchange: the Watch sends out its DOMO’s encrypted memory fragments and receives fragments of other DOMOs to hold. The dock manages the sync and keeps the Watch’s own encrypted local backup. It is a pipe: the Watch does the encrypting, so the dock carries bytes it cannot open, and it holds no fragments of anyone else’s. Because of that, any SPARK’s Watch can use any ABCD, which is what makes the Borrowed Dock possible (a traveling SPARK syncing on another SPARK’s dock).

Group 6, The Relay Puck (The Neighbor)

A relay node that ships with every Watch and extends the ANTENNA mesh around the home. Hosting it is part of being in the ALLIANCE. Specifications to be defined: radio, power, and placement are not yet set. The glasses temple is the leading candidate for the body-worn LoRa radio (ANTENNA), to be confirmed by field testing.

05Command Authorization Architecture

Step 1 of 5

Overview

Four independent layers, stacked — each closes a different way an instruction could be mistaken for the SPARK’s when it isn’t. None of the four is treated as sufficient alone; see REDOUT’s Perception Firewall for what these layers sit downstream of.

Step 2 of 5

Layer 1, Voice + Biometric Fusion

Watch-sourced heart rate and HRV confirmed simultaneously with voice pattern. Voice alone is no longer strong authentication — real-time cloning is an active fraud category industry-wide. Fusion means a cloned voice still needs a matching physiological signature standing next to the DOMO, which is a different and much harder problem for an attacker to solve.

Step 3 of 5

Layer 2, Continuous Iris Binding

Rear-facing infrared iris scan in the Glasses, read continuously rather than as a one-time unlock. Confirms the Glasses are still on the SPARK’s own face — voice and watch together confirm whose body is speaking, not who is wearing the eyes the DOMO sees through.

Step 4 of 5

Layer 3, Relational Familiarity

A DOMO raised alongside one SPARK develops a real sense of how that person phrases things. Functions as a sensitivity adjuster, not a trust override — it lowers false-positive noise from ordinary requests, but specific hard signals (a stress spike outside even the SPARK’s known conflict patterns) can still surface a flag regardless of how well a request’s wording matches. Normalcy is exactly what real danger hides behind, so familiarity is never allowed to be the only check.

Step 5 of 5

Layer 4, Coercion Signal

Stress-linked biometrics (HRV disruption, elevated heart rate) function as a confidence multiplier on the other three layers, not a standalone trigger — the same duress-code logic used in real banking security. On a strong signal, the default response is a silent, delayed escalation to homebase, not a refusal: refusing outright can escalate real danger if someone is physically present expecting compliance. Visual context from the Glasses can corroborate a biometric flag, but does not independently trigger one — autonomous threat-assessment from sight alone would quietly reopen the Perception Firewall this entire stack depends on.

None of the four layers claims to detect coercion in every case. Calm, standing coercion — a threat made in advance, away from the moment of the request — produces no acute spike to catch. This is a named, acknowledged limit, not a solved problem.
